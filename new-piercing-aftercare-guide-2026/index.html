AFTERCARE

The Updated Aftercare Guide

Cleaning, healing timelines, common mistakes, and infection signs every new piercing needs.

Published: August 17, 2026

THE STORY

Why Aftercare Mistakes Are So Common

Proper aftercare remains one of the most important — and most commonly mishandled — parts of getting a new piercing. Despite piercing's long-standing popularity in the U.S., misinformation about cleaning routines, healing expectations, and infection signs continues to circulate, often passed along informally rather than sourced from piercing professionals or medical guidance.

This matters to U.S. readers because aftercare mistakes are among the leading contributors to piercing complications, according to piercing industry professionals, and many of these issues are preventable with accurate information. As piercing styles diversify — from simple lobes to more complex cartilage placements — aftercare needs vary more than many first-time piercing recipients realize.

The following reflects widely shared guidance from piercing industry sources such as the Association of Professional Piercers. It is not a substitute for personalized medical advice, and anyone with concerns about their piercing should consult a healthcare provider.

CLEANING

Cleaning Recommendations

Saline Solution

A sterile saline solution — either a pre-made piercing aftercare spray or a solution mixed at an appropriate saline-to-water ratio — is the cleaning method most commonly recommended by piercing professionals. It's generally suggested to clean the piercing a couple of times a day, though exact frequency recommendations can vary by piercer, so following the specific aftercare sheet given at the time of piercing is advisable.

What to Avoid

Piercing professionals commonly advise against using alcohol, hydrogen peroxide, or antibacterial ointments on new piercings, as these can be overly harsh on healing tissue and, in the case of ointments, can trap bacteria against the piercing site. Rotating jewelry unnecessarily during cleaning is also generally discouraged, as it can irritate the healing channel rather than help it.

MISTAKES

Common Aftercare Mistakes

Over-Cleaning

Cleaning a piercing too frequently or too aggressively can irritate the area and slow healing, according to piercer guidance — more is not necessarily better when it comes to saline application.

Using Household Products

Products like rubbing alcohol, tea tree oil, or homemade saltwater mixes with incorrect concentrations are commonly cited by piercers as causes of irritation, and some can be harsh enough to damage healing tissue.

Touching with Unwashed Hands

Handling a new piercing without washing hands first is a frequently cited source of introduced bacteria, according to piercing aftercare guidance.

Removing Jewelry Too Early

Piercing professionals generally advise against removing jewelry before a piercing is fully healed, since piercing channels — especially cartilage ones — can shrink or close quickly, sometimes within hours.

HEALING TIME

General Healing Timelines

Healing time varies significantly by piercing location and individual factors. As a general reference commonly cited in piercing industry guidance: earlobe piercings often take around 6 to 8 weeks to heal, while cartilage piercings can take several months to a year or longer. These are general ranges rather than guarantees, and actual healing time depends on aftercare, individual health, and piercing placement.

WARNING SIGNS

Recognizing Infection Signs

Piercing professionals and health resources commonly note that some redness, mild swelling, and clear or slightly whitish discharge can be normal in the initial healing period. Signs more commonly associated with infection include increasing redness spreading beyond the piercing site, significant swelling, warmth, persistent pain, or yellow/green discharge with an odor. Fever accompanying piercing-site symptoms is generally considered a signal to seek medical attention promptly.

When to See a Professional

Anyone experiencing symptoms that seem to worsen rather than improve, or who develops fever, should consult a healthcare provider rather than attempting to self-treat. A piercer can offer guidance related to jewelry or technique, but cannot diagnose infection — that determination should come from a medical professional.

Note: This is general aftercare information, not medical advice. If you suspect an infection, contact a healthcare provider.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I clean a new piercing?

Most piercing professionals recommend saline cleaning once or twice daily, but follow the specific instructions provided by your piercer.

Can I use hydrogen peroxide on a piercing?

No — piercing professionals generally advise against hydrogen peroxide and alcohol, as they can be too harsh on healing tissue.

How long does it take for a piercing to fully heal?

It varies by location: earlobes often heal in 6-8 weeks, while cartilage piercings can take several months to over a year.

What are signs my piercing might be infected?

Spreading redness, significant swelling, warmth, persistent pain, or discolored discharge with odor are commonly cited signs to have evaluated by a professional.

Should I remove jewelry if I think it's infected?

Consult a healthcare provider before removing jewelry, since removing it can sometimes trap infection inside a closing channel.
