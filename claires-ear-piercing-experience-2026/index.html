Claire's Reinvents Ear Piercing for U.S. Shoppers

New protocols, staff training, and jewelry options across U.S. and Canada stores mark a shift for the mall-piercing giant.

Published: August 17, 2026

THE STORY

A Legacy Retailer Responds to a More Informed Shopper

Claire's, the mall-based jewelry and accessories retailer long associated with first ear piercings for children and teens, has been updating its in-store piercing experience across the United States and Canada. The changes come as the retailer faces new competition from piercing studios, dermatologists offering piercing services, and a generation of consumers who research hygiene standards and jewelry materials online before booking an appointment.

For decades, Claire's built its identity on convenience — walk-in piercings performed by store associates, often for a child's first set of earrings. But heightened consumer awareness around piercing safety, driven partly by social media discussions about infections and metal sensitivities, has pushed the company to publicly emphasize training and equipment upgrades rather than relying solely on its legacy reputation.

This shift matters to American readers because Claire's remains one of the most widely used entry points into piercing in the U.S., particularly for young children. Parents deciding where to take a child, or where to get a first piercing themselves, are increasingly comparing mall retailers against professional studios — making transparency about protocols and materials a real factor in that decision.

WHAT'S CHANGING

What Claire's Says Is Changing

Staff Training

Claire's has said that piercing associates undergo company-run certification training before performing piercings in stores. The company has highlighted this training as a way to standardize technique and hygiene practices across its large store footprint, which spans hundreds of mall locations in the U.S. and Canada.

Piercing Devices

Claire's has historically used spring-loaded piercing guns, a method that differs from the sterile, single-use needles preferred by professional piercing studios. The company continues to market gun-based piercing as fast and low-pain, positioning it as appropriate for earlobe piercings in particular, while studios and piercing associations have long argued that needle piercing causes less tissue trauma. This is an ongoing point of debate in the piercing industry rather than a settled consensus.

Jewelry Options

The retailer has expanded jewelry offerings marketed for sensitive ears, including options described as hypoallergenic or surgical-stainless-steel. As with any retailer claim, shoppers should verify specific material certifications (such as ASTM F136 titanium or ISO-certified surgical steel) rather than relying on marketing terms like "hypoallergenic" alone, since that term is not strictly regulated in the U.S.

In-Store Experience

Claire's has also updated store layouts and consultation processes in some locations, aiming to make the piercing area feel more clinical and less like a walk-up counter. This includes visible sanitation stations and printed aftercare instructions handed to customers post-piercing.

CONTEXT

Why This Matters for U.S. Consumers

The piercing industry in the U.S. is not uniformly regulated at the federal level; oversight varies by state and sometimes by county health department. That patchwork means a retailer's internal protocols can meaningfully affect the actual safety of a piercing experience, since there isn't always a strong external regulatory backstop. Consumers researching Claire's changes should treat company statements as claims to verify — for example, by asking in-store staff directly about single-use needle cartridges versus reusable gun components, sterilization procedures, and jewelry material certifications.

Industry professionals affiliated with organizations like the Association of Professional Piercers (APP) have historically recommended needle piercing over gun piercing for cartilage and non-lobe piercings, citing tissue trauma concerns. Claire's updates do not appear to change its core method for most piercings, according to public reporting, so readers seeking cartilage piercings may still want to consider a specialized studio.

What to Ask Before Booking

  • Is the piercing device single-use or reusable, and how is it sterilized between customers?
  • What certification does the piercing jewelry carry (implant-grade titanium, ASTM F136, or nickel-free confirmation)?
  • Has the piercing associate completed a specific certification program, and how recently?
  • What aftercare instructions are provided, and are they consistent with current professional guidance?

Note: This article reflects publicly available company statements and general piercing-industry guidance. It is not medical advice — consult a healthcare provider with specific health concerns.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Claire's use needles or piercing guns?

Claire's has traditionally used piercing guns for most ear piercings; check with your local store for current equipment used for specific piercing types.

Is Claire's safe for children's first piercings?

Safety depends on staff training, sterilization practices, and jewelry quality at the specific location — ask store staff about their current protocols.

What jewelry material is safest for new piercings?

Implant-grade titanium and certified surgical steel are commonly recommended by piercing professionals to reduce reaction risk.

Are Claire's changes the same in every store?

Protocols can vary by location and country, so it's worth confirming specifics with your local store before booking.

Should I choose a studio instead of a mall retailer?

For cartilage or more complex piercings, many piercing professionals recommend specialized studios using needle techniques.
